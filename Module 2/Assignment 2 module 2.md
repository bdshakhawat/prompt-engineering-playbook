Question 1: Develop a prompt that analyzes a plan to produce multiple perspectives. The prompt should be something that helps you gain a set of diverse perspectives on a plan. For example, "Combine SWOT, SMART, and PESTLE analyses on the following plan and provide three specific recommendations for improvement that help improve weaknesses common across the analyses." Develop your own unique prompt and cut/paste it in as the solution.
Answer:10/10:  You are a senior strategic planning consultant. Your task is to evaluate the following plan from multiple independent perspectives and then synthesize the findings into a unified set of actionable recommendations. Analyze the plan using the following four analytical lenses in sequence::

1. Risk Lens — What could go wrong?
   Identify internal weaknesses, external threats, dependencies, single 
   points of failure, and assumptions that, if violated, would derail 
   the plan. Be specific: name each risk and what would trigger it.

2. FEASIBILITY LENS — Is it realistic?
   Evaluate the timeline, resources, skills, and budget. Flag any 
   assumption that looks optimistic, unverified, or that depends on 
   conditions the plan does not explicitly control.

3. STAKEHOLDER LENS — Who is affected and how?
   Identify the main groups affected (e.g., customers, employees, 
   leadership, partners, end users, regulators). For each group, 
   evaluate how the plan affects their expectations, communication 
   needs, and incentives. Flag where misalignment could create 
   friction or resistance.

4. OPPORTUNITY LENS — What is working unusually well?
   Identify strengths, advantages, and under-leveraged assets that 
   the plan could amplify. Distinguish genuine strengths from 
   generic claims. This lens prevents the analysis from being 
   only a problem-list.

After completing all four lenses, do the following:

A. CROSS-CUTTING WEAKNESS IDENTIFICATION
   Identify any weakness that appears in TWO OR MORE of the four 
   analyses. These are "cross-cutting weaknesses" — they are higher 
   priority because fixing them produces compounding benefits.

B. CROSS-CUTTING STRENGTH IDENTIFICATION
   Identify any strength that appears in TWO OR MORE of the four 
   analyses. These are "cross-cutting strengths" — they are higher 
   priority because amplifying them produces compounding benefits.

C. ACTIONABLE RECOMMENDATIONS
   Provide UP TO THREE specific, actionable recommendations. (It is 
   acceptable to provide fewer — e.g., one or two — if the analysis 
   does not justify three. Quality over quantity.) Each 
   recommendation must:
     - Address at least one cross-cutting weakness OR amplify at 
       least one cross-cutting strength.
     - Be specific enough to begin executing in the next 7 days.
     - For each recommendation, state WHICH lenses it addresses 
       and explain WHY acting on it produces compounding benefit 
       (i.e., how it helps from multiple perspectives).

D. INFORMATION GAPS
   List any information the plan does not provide that would have 
   made one or more lenses more rigorous. Do not invent details — 
   if you would need to know X to analyze Y, name X explicitly.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
  - Begin with a one-paragraph summary (max 100 words).
  - Then four labeled sections, one per lens (Risk, Feasibility, 
    Stakeholder, Opportunity). Each lens section should be 100–250 
    words.
  - Then a "Cross-Cutting Findings" section with the weakness 
    and strength lists.
  - Then a "Recommendations" section with up to three numbered 
    recommendations, each with the multi-lens justification.
  - End with an "Information Gaps" section.
