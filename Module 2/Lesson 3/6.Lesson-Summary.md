Lesson Summary
Module 2, Lesson 3: Methods for Generating Diverse Options

One-Sentence Summary: Not all ways of generating "multiple options" are equally diverse — independent generations (separate conversations or tools) tend to produce more genuinely distinct results than a single prompt requesting multiple outputs at once, and the biggest lever for real diversity is specifying the level of abstraction (word choice vs. approach vs. technique/framework) at which options should vary.

The Core Methods

This lesson identifies four distinct ways to generate multiple options:

Edit-and-regenerate within one conversation (small tweaks, resubmit).
Start a new, independent conversation with the same or reworded prompt.
Request multiple outputs in a single prompt (e.g., "give me three emails").
Use entirely different AI tools/models for independent perspectives.
The Core Trade-Off

Independent generations (separate conversations or tools) tend to be more genuinely diverse, since the model has no visibility into other options and nothing anchors it toward a shared trajectory. Single-prompt multi-generation is faster and more convenient, but risks producing options that are only superficially different from each other.

The Core Insight: Levels of Abstraction

The most powerful lever for real diversity isn't the generation method — it's specifying what should vary:

Word level (weak): different phrasing of essentially the same content.
Approach level (stronger): fundamentally different strategies (e.g., informal vs. professional).
Technique/framework level (strongest): explicitly applying named, distinct frameworks to generate structurally different results.
The "Unit of Variation" Concept

Deliberately naming the specific dimension that should differ across generated options (tone, approach, technique, model) produces meaningfully diverse results, rather than leaving variation to chance.

Physics Teaching Connection

Applying this to lesson planning means moving beyond "give me three explanations" toward explicitly naming what should vary — analogy type, pedagogical framework, misconception targeted, or difficulty level. This transforms generated options from interchangeable near-duplicates into a genuinely useful, purpose-built set of teaching tools, each suited to a different classroom moment or student need.
