# Physics Prompt Templates

> A reusable collection of Physics-focused prompt templates for teaching, explanation, problem solving, misconception analysis, content creation, AI evaluation, and Physics AI training workflows.

---

## Purpose

This library translates general Prompt Engineering patterns into reusable templates for Physics-related tasks.

The templates are designed to support:

- Physics education
- Physics content development
- AI-assisted teaching
- Physics AI evaluation
- AI training and data-quality work
- Physics Subject Matter Expert (SME) workflows
- Student misconception analysis
- Educational content generation
- Physics problem analysis

These are **templates, not fixed prompts**.

Replace the placeholders with task-specific information before using them.

---

20. Physics Multi-Perspective Analysis Template
Purpose

Analyze a Physics-related educational or AI problem from multiple perspectives.

Template
Act as a Physics SME and educational analysis assistant.

PROBLEM / PLAN:
[INPUT]

Analyze the input using four distinct lenses.

1. SCIENTIFIC LENS
Evaluate scientific accuracy, assumptions, principles, and technical validity.

2. EDUCATIONAL LENS
Evaluate conceptual clarity, learner needs, misconceptions, and instructional usefulness.

3. PRACTICAL LENS
Evaluate feasibility, available resources, time, and implementation challenges.

4. AI / EVALUATION LENS
Evaluate how reliably an AI system could perform or support the task and identify potential failure modes.

After completing the four analyses:

## Cross-Cutting Findings

Identify issues or strengths appearing across multiple lenses.

## Recommendations

Provide up to three actionable recommendations.

## Information Gaps

Identify information required for a more rigorous analysis.

 Is the target audience specified when relevant?

33. Template Maintenance

When improving a template:

Record the original version.
Identify the observed failure.
Modify only the necessary components.
Test the revised version.
Compare the outputs.
Document important changes.

Avoid continuously adding instructions without evidence that they improve performance.
